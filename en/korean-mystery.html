KOREA · UNSOLVED CASE ARCHIVE 001

The Daegu Frog Boys Case

Five missing children. Remains found eleven years later. Questions that remain unanswered.

Case overview

On March 26, 1991, five elementary-school boys went to Mount Waryong in Daegu, South Korea, and did not return home. Extensive searches followed, but their whereabouts remained unknown. On September 26, 2002, the remains of all five boys were found on the mountain. The circumstances of their deaths and the person or persons responsible have not been established.

The victims were children, and their families have endured decades of grief. This page avoids sensational reenactments and unsupported accusations.

Documented timeline

March 26, 1991
Five boys went to Mount Waryong and went missing.

Following years
Large-scale searches were conducted, but the boys were not found.

September 26, 2002
The remains of the five boys were discovered on Mount Waryong.

March 2006
The statute of limitations then applicable to the case expired.

2019–2020
Police reviewed case records and submitted more than 100 retained items for forensic examination. A June 2020 report said investigators had not obtained a meaningful new lead from those examinations at that time.

What remains unknown?

  • The precise circumstances and cause of the boys' deaths
  • The identity of any person responsible
  • How the physical evidence should be interpreted, and its limitations

Online theories about specific weapons, methods or suspects are not established findings. They are not repeated here as facts.
